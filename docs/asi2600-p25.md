# ASI2600MM Pro P25

The attached 2025 camera reports `ZWO ASI2600MM Pro` through SDK 1.41.
Its USB interface is `03c3:260e`, USB3, bulk IN `81`, with 1024-byte packets
and burst 15. The mono sensor is 6248 × 4176, 16-bit, 3.76 µm, with bins 1–4.
Direct support is restricted to this observed interface. Other ASI2600 models
are not identified by their name alone.

The SDK remains the default. NINA and Alpaca can select this camera with the
direct driver and optional SDK fallback. Cooler recovery, dew, fan and LED
control checks passed on this unit. Measured frames have dark-frame statistics.

## What changed

The P25 shares the original ASI2600 RAW16 sensor table, gain mapping and ASID
defect-map format. It has its own volatile initialization table, HMAX 790
instead of 779, and FPGA divisor 400 instead of 3. No firmware or EEPROM
writes are used. Calibration is read from each camera rather than shipped
with the driver.

Two transfer differences needed fixes:

- The ready flag can appear before all sensor rows reach camera memory.
  A fixed 100 ms delay produced new pixels in the upper rows and old pixels
  below, despite valid frame markers. The P25 path waits one programmed
  sensor frame interval plus 100 ms before freezing the frame. Full-frame
  offset transitions check every row's median for this failure.
- A 64 × 64 retained transfer stalled; 512 × 128 worked. Small regions now
  use at least 128 KiB of sensor readout, apply defect correction at the
  actual sensor origin, then crop and bin to the requested dimensions.

The retained-frame sender stops before the host resets its USB pipe. A partial
read may need two restarts. The driver rereads from byte zero and checks the
frame envelope; tests compare the recovered pixels with the interrupted
prefix and a complete second read. A new exposure still requires permission
from the configured recapture duration limit, which defaults to 30 seconds.

## Evidence

The Release direct worker passed 39 cases and 41 frames, including full-frame
offset changes, bins 1–4, small/moved/edge regions, gain boundaries, exposures
through 60 seconds and three transfer-fault cases. The interrupted first read
needed two retries; the forced USB timeout needed one. A partial replay also
recovered after one additional timeout. All replay pixels matched.
See [sanitized results](asi2600-p25-evidence.json).

NINA 3.2 displayed a complete 1,200-second direct exposure with no SDK loaded,
retry or fallback. Full frames at bins 1–4, a small region, a short exposure
loop and capture after cancellation also passed. Killing the direct worker
during a 10-second exposure caused a five-second reconnect delay and one
replacement exposure. NINA received and displayed the recovered image; the
retry appeared in its log. Cooling and dew heating were off throughout.

The Rust Alpaca server passed bins 1–4, cancellation and a subsequent capture
in SDK, direct and direct-with-fallback configurations. Terminating its worker
during an exposure recovered in all three configurations. With fallback
enabled, the replacement used the SDK; otherwise it kept the selected backend.
Gain 100 and offset 50 were restored after each worker failure.

Ten additional full-frame offset transitions passed the stricter check of
every row's median, across 32 µs, 100 ms, 999,999 µs, 1 second and 2 seconds.

SDK 1.41 passed all 47 Camera Kit exercises, including bins 1–4, moved/edge
regions, gain −25 through 700, offset 0 through 240, USB bandwidth changes
and exposures through 60 seconds. Flip, hardware binning and high-speed
toggles passed in the SDK kit; the direct path uses normal RAW16 and software
binning only.

Independent Rust processing matched all 52,183,296 output bytes from the
same full-frame USB capture, including 26,091 factory defect indices. It also
matched all 43 normal-mode Camera Kit samples, covering bins, regions,
exposure settings and control changes. These compare identical source pixels,
not two separate exposures. Raw traces, serials, calibration and images stay
local; published evidence contains only metadata and digests.

The timing and gain unit tests use SDK register observations from the kit.
The initialization extractor requires the exact reviewed trace SHA-256.
Cold power-up, physical USB removal, illuminated exposure timing and Linux/macOS
hardware testing remain open. The readout guard is time based; a definitive
firmware indication that every row has finished is not yet known.

## Cooler and auxiliary controls

The SDK probe cooled the sensor from 30.6 °C to 26.6 °C in 30 seconds.
Three Alpaca tests then killed the camera worker during a five-second exposure
while cooling, with dew enabled and LED brightness set to 128. All returned a
replacement frame and restored the target, cooler enable, dew, fan and LED.

| Recovery path | Target restored | Prior temperature | Temperature when allowed to retry | Time to returned image |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SDK → SDK | 19 °C | 23.2 °C | 22.8 °C | 45.3 s |
| Direct → direct | 15 °C | 24.0 °C | 22.6 °C | 14.6 s |
| Direct → SDK fallback | 14 °C | 22.7 °C | 19.0 °C | 89.3 s |

Recovery restored the setpoint but did not wait to reach it. It required three
acceptable samples, two seconds apart, of temperature and cooler output.
The SDK restarted its output at zero, so fallback waited longer for power to
recover. Temperatures closer to the target than the prior reading are accepted.

A separate NINA test interrupted a ten-second full-frame exposure at 18 °C
and 59% cooler output, with a 10 °C target and dew enabled. The reopened cooler
started at zero output. It reached the target using less power than before,
so the alternative recovery rule accepted 30 seconds within 1 °C of the target,
followed by three accepted samples. It resumed at 9.5 °C / 41% and returned the
6248 × 4176 image after 146.9 seconds. NINA displayed the frame without an error
dialog (mean 500.77, SD 21.75 ADU). Thus a high prior output can extend recovery
until the target is held; recovery does not always finish near the prior
temperature. No driver changes were needed for these tests.

Fan and LED commands at 0, 128 and 255 matched hardware register readbacks.
Dew toggling matched both the enable bit and output register (0 or 197).
The passive trace recorded 444 completed environment requests with no USB or
driver status errors. These checks verify control commands and state; they do
not measure fan RPM, light output or window temperature.

Starting controls were restored, leaving cooler and dew off, fan and LED at 255.
See [thermal test evidence](asi2600-p25-thermal-evidence.json) and the repeatable
[validation script](../scripts/inspection/validate_environment.py). Faults were
worker terminations, not power loss or cable removal.

## Repeat the tests

Disconnect the camera in other apps first and cap it for the offset checks.
Use a new output filename for each run. The matrix saves statistics, not images.

```powershell
cargo build --release --locked
target/release/regain-device.exe zwo camera-direct --capture-2600-p25 --gain 100 --offset 50 --replay
python scripts/inspection/validate_asi2600_p25.py --worker target/release/regain-device.exe --output artifacts/NEW-2600-p25.jsonl
# Only the full-frame freshness checks:
python scripts/inspection/validate_asi2600_p25.py --worker target/release/regain-device.exe --output artifacts/NEW-2600-p25-rows.jsonl --full-rows-only
# Add a real 20-minute full-frame exposure:
python scripts/inspection/validate_asi2600_p25.py --worker target/release/regain-device.exe --output artifacts/NEW-2600-p25-long.jsonl --long 1200
```

The Python validator needs NumPy. It rejects stale full-frame rows, wrong
dimensions, digest mismatches, failed retained replays and duplicate frames.
