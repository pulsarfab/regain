# ASI6200MM Pro P25

Testing uses one capped ASI6200MM Pro, identified by its owner as the 2025/P25
edition. The SDK reports `ZWO ASI6200MM Pro`; the observed USB interface is
`03c3:620b`, USB 3, bulk IN endpoint `81`, 1024-byte packets and burst 15.
The sensor is monochrome, 9576 × 6388, 16-bit, 3.76 µm, with bins 1–4.
The SDK remains the default. The direct backend accepts this specific PID and
USB interface; this is not evidence for other ASI6200 variants or P25 models.

## Acquisition and processing

The SDK 1.41 baseline passed 47 Camera Kit exercises: full frame, moved and
edge ROIs, bins 1–4, exposure boundaries through 60 seconds, gain/offset/USB
controls, flip, hardware-bin and high-speed toggles. The plugin's still-image
path uses RAW16, software binning, no flip and normal speed.

The Rust processor independently decodes this camera's ASID factory map. All
122,342,976 bytes of a full-frame result matched the SDK's result for the
**same USB frame**, including 61,171 defect indices. ROI and bins 2–4 also
matched. Calibration and pixel payloads stay local and are not embedded in
the driver or committed to the repository.

An additional offline comparison reconstructed the wire frames from 43 Camera
Kit samples and passed each through Rust's `--process-frame` path. Every output
byte matched the corresponding SDK sample, covering moved/edge ROIs, bins 1–4,
32 µs–60 s exposure settings, gain/offset sweeps and both USB bandwidth variants.
Flip, hardware-bin and high-speed samples were excluded from this RAW16 normal-
mode processing comparison. Their SDK exercises passed separately.

This model has its own volatile initialization and RAW16 tables. Reproduce
them with `scripts/inspection/extract_asi6200_tables.py` and the pinned trace.
Important differences from the ASI2600 include:

- Sensor standby gate `B6:019e`; ROI origin `a6/a7` and `06/07`, dimensions
  `08/09` and `018c/018d`, shutter `16/17`.
- HMAX 880, 20 MHz clock, 52 blanking rows and FPGA divisor 400 on this unit.
  Short exposure timing uses single-precision arithmetic; host-timed exposures
  start at one second and use a separate gating sequence.
- Gain 0–700, conversion/readout transitions at 61, 100, 160 and 280,
  and digital gain stages above 460. Offset 0–200 writes two 16-bit registers
  scaled by ten. All gain transition register values have independent SDK
  trace fixtures in the Rust tests.
- Small retained frames of 8, 16, 32 and 64 KiB stalled during replay. Both
  narrow and wide 128 KiB frames worked. The direct backend therefore pads
  small physical ROIs to at least 128 KiB, corrects defects at the actual
  sensor origin, crops, then bins. NINA receives its requested dimensions.

Full-frame control transitions exposed a lifecycle bug missed by repeated dark
frames at constant settings: `BC:23=15` can precede completion of sensor readout.
Stopping after a fixed 100 ms refreshed only part of the 6388 rows, despite valid
frame markers and matching replay bytes. The direct path now allows a complete
programmed sensor frame interval plus a settling margin before standby. Short
mode discards the queued streaming pass and reads frozen DDR; long mode consumes
its scheduled initial pass. `BD:00` bit 4 must not be set during preservation on
this unit; it clears retained state. This guard is time based. A definitive
firmware completion indicator and illuminated timing validation remain open.
The `--full-controls` regression option alternates full-frame offsets and saves
16 vertical-band profiles to detect stale rows. Earlier constant-control image
tests alone did not establish whole-frame freshness.

The direct hardware matrix has passed full-frame repeats, bins 1–4, moved
and edge ROIs, gain boundaries, offset endpoints, and exposures from 32 µs
through 60 seconds. See `scripts/inspection/validate_asi6200.py` for the matrix.
A full-resolution 1,200-second direct exposure completed in NINA with SDK
fallback disabled. Exposure and readout took 1,202.17 seconds; the image pane
displayed all 9576 × 6388 pixels, mean 970.18 ADU and standard deviation 582.00
ADU. The sensor finished at 24.9°C against a 25°C target. The advertised maximum
is 2,000 seconds; a complete hardware run at that maximum has not been performed.

## Transfer recovery

The retained DDR frame can be read again without repeating the exposure.
The driver checks both envelope markers and matching counters; a replay's
counter may differ from the original while its interior pixels stay identical.

After a partial transfer, the first restart can itself stall. The same-frame
retry budget handles that additional timeout. Three full-frame tests interrupted
a replay after 12 MiB, then recovered identical pixels following one real
five-second read timeout each. A 60-second full-frame exposure recovered after
an actual `CancelIoEx` on the thirteenth bulk request, followed by a read timeout;
both retries stayed within the default budget of two. A subsequent complete
replay matched all interior pixel bytes. The trace recorded exactly one `A9`
exposure-start command and no loaded SDK. These are controlled faults, not a
physical USB unplug test or naturally occurring device errors.

Two-second full-frame tests also canceled the first and last (117th) bulk
requests. The first-chunk case recovered after two read retries in 14.012 seconds;
the last-chunk case needed one retry and completed in 9.548 seconds. Each issued
one exposure-start command and passed complete retained-replay pixel comparison.

The 30-second default cutoff applies to a **new exposure**, not a same-frame
read. Disconnects that lose the retained frame still require a permitted full
recapture or an error for a longer exposure.

## Environment and options

The temperature, cooler output, enable and dew registers match the mapped
ASI2600 interface. SDK cooling lowered the measured temperature from 30.5°C to
24.5°C in a 60-second test at a 20°C target. The independent Rust regulator is
serviced during idle, exposure and transfer.

Fan speed and power-LED brightness accept 0–255 and map to FPGA `fa` and `fb`.
Both are checked against hardware registers after each write and restored across recovery. Optional setup values
apply on connect; blank fields preserve the current camera value. The USB hub
reset is a momentary SDK operation and is deliberately excluded from automatic
state restoration. It is not exposed by the direct backend.

The real-camera `CameraSession` recovery test killed the direct worker at
download with a 20°C target, 26.1°C measured temperature and 27% cooler output.
After the five-second reconnect delay it restored all controls, accepted three
readings near the previous temperature (25.3, 25.1, 24.6°C), and recaptured once.
The 20°C target, dew heater, fan speed and LED brightness survived. Cancelling
a subsequent 60-second exposure and taking another frame also recovered.

The same `CameraSession` harness separately passed primary-SDK recovery and
direct-to-SDK fallback with the real cooler. Primary SDK recovery restored a
15°C target from 19.8°C and 19% output; the replacement frame completed near
20.3°C after the SDK regulator regained the required output. Fallback restored
a 20°C target from 24.5°C and 20% output, and completed at 21.6°C and 10% output.
Each run took exactly one replacement exposure, preserved gain 280, offset 50,
dew on, fan 200 and LED 128, and passed cancellation followed by another capture.
These are hardware-backed supervisor tests, separate from NINA UI tests.

A later NINA fallback test found that the old output threshold could stall
recovery at a healthy setpoint: direct cooldown used 25%, whereas the SDK held
20°C with 6–9%. Recovery now also accepts 30 seconds of sustained regulation at
the restored target, followed by the configured stable samples. Missing or
interrupted telemetry, zero cooler output and a steadily warming sensor reset
that hold. This does not require reaching the target when the normal prior-
temperature and output criteria already pass.

See the [NINA image-pane matrix](nina-end-to-end.md#asi6200mm-pro-p25-2026-09-14).
Dark frames establish transport, dimensions, processing consistency and
recovery behavior; they do not establish illuminated-image quality.
